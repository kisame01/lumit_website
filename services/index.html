01 — Assess

Migrate

Cloud Strategy, Architecture & Migration

Deciding what should move, how it should move, and whether moving it improves anything. Then building the foundation it lands on.

The problem

You have been told to move to the cloud. Nobody has said which workloads should move, how they should move, or what gets better afterwards. Meanwhile the environment already running in Azure or AWS has grown without consistent identity, network, policy, tagging or logging standards, and nobody is confident it could be rebuilt if it had to be.

What we do

We assess each workload against six options and put numbers on both sides of every decision. We then design or repair the foundation those workloads land on: subscription and account structure, identity and privileged access, network topology, policy guardrails, logging and monitoring, secrets and key management, backup and recovery patterns, and a tagging standard that makes cost allocation possible once the invoices start arriving.

What you get

  • A workload disposition matrix, with the reasoning recorded against each decision
  • Target-state architecture for the platform and its landing zone
  • A sequenced migration plan with dependencies, cutover approach and rollback positions
  • Infrastructure defined as code, with deployment pipelines and operational runbooks
  • An investment case a CFO can take to the board
02 — Pipelines
Integration
Platform

Data Architecture, Engineering & Integration

The connective work that decides whether everything above it succeeds. It is also the part that gets under-scoped.

The problem

Reporting and AI ambitions are usually blocked lower down than anyone admits. The data sits in an ERP with no API, a database nobody wants to touch, a nightly file drop that fails quietly, and four systems that each hold a different version of the same customer.

What we do

We map the dependencies before designing anything: what feeds what, on what schedule, through which credential, and what breaks when it fails. Then we build the connective layer — scheduled extracts, change data capture, secure file exchange, queues with retry behaviour, and APIs in front of systems that never had one. Monitoring tells you a load failed before a business user does.

What you get

  • A dependency and integration map of the current estate
  • Ingestion and transformation pipelines in version control, with automated tests
  • Data platform architecture sized to the actual workload
  • Monitoring, alerting and documented failure and recovery behaviour
  • A defined path to scale, and the measurable conditions that should trigger it
03 — Models
Definitions
Ownership

Data Warehousing, Reporting & Governance

Reports disagree. Month-end takes three days of manual reconciliation. The usual instinct is to buy a better reporting tool, but the cause is almost always underneath it.

The problem

Nobody can say who owns the definition of an active customer, and two reports give two answers to the same question.

What we do

We design and optimise warehouses, dimensional models, business rules and reporting layers so that a number has one definition and one place it comes from. Where a model is slow, we establish whether the cause is design, volume or infrastructure before anyone spends money on it. Governance goes in at the minimum that makes data trustworthy — domains and owners, a business glossary, quality rules that run on their own, lineage, access classification and POPIA-aligned handling. The rest waits until the first part is being used.

What you get

  • Consistent figures across reports, with a documented definition for every measure that matters
  • Named owners for the data that matters
  • Automated data quality rules that catch failures in the pipeline, not in a board pack
  • A defensible position on where personal data lives and who can reach it
  • Faster processing, with a stated reason for the improvement
04 — Visibility
Right-sizing
FinOps

Cloud Cost Management & Optimisation

Without visibility and active management, cloud spend grows faster than the value it delivers.

The problem

The invoice arrives and nobody can explain it by workload, environment or department. Underused resources keep running because nobody owns them.

What we do

We establish visibility of cloud expenditure across subscriptions, accounts, workloads, environments, departments and services. We identify over-provisioned compute and storage, review consumption patterns to remove waste, and introduce budgets, tagging, ownership models and forecasting. Then we help technology and finance teams work together around cloud economics rather than around a spreadsheet.

What you get

  • Cost visibility broken down by workload, environment, department and service
  • A ranked optimisation backlog with effort and expected saving stated per item
  • Budgets, tagging standards and cost ownership that survive the engagement
  • Forecasting based on usage trends and workload patterns
  • A FinOps operating rhythm shared between technology and finance
05 — Orchestration
Automation
Register

Operations, DevOps & Estate Assessment

Keeping what has been built running predictably, and being able to say quickly what the organisation actually owns.

The problem

Jobs fail at two in the morning and someone finds out at nine. Deployments are manual, undocumented, and done by one person who is now on leave. Most organisations also cannot quickly answer what they own, what is unsupported, what is idle, where sensitive data sits, and whether the backups have ever actually been restored.

What we do

We improve scheduling, dependency management, monitoring, automation and deployment practice. Orchestration runs on Apache Airflow or the scheduler your platform already supports. Environments are defined as code in Terraform or Bicep, so they can be rebuilt from a repository rather than reconstructed from memory, and pipelines run through GitHub Actions. Alerts go to a named person holding a runbook. Alongside that we run a structured assessment of the application, cloud, data, integration, infrastructure, security, cost and governance landscape — by people with no product to sell you at the end of it.

What you get

  • A technology estate register with named owners and business criticality
  • Current-state architecture and dependency map
  • A risk and remediation register, ranked by cost, risk and criticality
  • Recovery findings that separate what has been tested from what has been assumed
  • A quick-win plan, a twelve-month roadmap and an indicative investment case
06 — Register
Risk
Oversight

AI Readiness & Governed Delivery

We do not sell AI as a product line. There are two practical questions: whether you are ready to put AI into production, and whether the software we build for you is safe when AI has helped build it.

The problem

Employees and development teams are already using AI. Management has no inventory of where, no policy, no risk classification, and no route from an experiment to something that can be operated in production. The data underneath the ambition is usually not ready either.

What we do

We build a use-case register, assess data readiness honestly, classify each use by risk, define where human oversight is required, and set out what has to be true before a use case goes into production. Governance material draws on POPIA-aligned data handling, recognised AI risk-management frameworks and applicable sector requirements.

What you get

  • An AI use-case register with risk classification per use
  • An honest data-readiness assessment against each intended use
  • Defined human oversight points and production entry criteria
  • Governance material aligned to POPIA and recognised AI risk frameworks
  • A delivery standard for AI-assisted engineering your security team can audit

This is technical and governance consulting, not legal advice.
